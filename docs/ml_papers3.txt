EXECUTIVE SUMMARY (read this first; details per paper below)
-------------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Goal: the best small LM we can train on a 3070 Ti 8 GB / Colab T4, with FAR FEWER params than
usual for its quality class. The two strongest levers found this sweep:

  (1) Parameter reuse at equal compute. Hyperloop (B2) matches or beats a depth-matched
      Transformer with ~45-57% of the params. Attractor Models (E1) beat a param-matched
      Transformer by ~15% val ppl at 140M with constant training memory in depth. The
      iso-depth scaling law (B1, phi = 0.46; 0.65 with hyper-connections) lets the planner
      price these trades honestly.
  (2) Nearly-free per-layer upgrades validated AT OUR SCALE: LayerRoPE (C1, -0.07 loss at
      134M, fewer params, 3-10x wider LR basin), value residual / SATFormer (C2), sink logit +
      value gate (E2), front-loaded FFN widths (F1).
  Plus optimizer upgrades on top of Muon: SAMuon-lite (A2, 13-24% fewer tokens), AF-Muon for
  tied embeddings (A4), ORCA (A3), scaled WD (A5), and the planner rules from A1/D1.

Recommended ablation order (each one vs the previous best, same token budget, 2+ seeds at
~30-60M to start; the cheapest and most certain come first):
   1. Baseline: RoPE/DaRoPE + RMSNorm + SwiGLU + QK-norm + tied emb + Muon(+AdamW) + WSD
   2. + LayerRoPE (C1)                       ~40 lines, 0 FLOPs
   3. + value residual (C2), then SATFormer gate
   4. + sink logit (+ proj value gate at >=100M) (E2)
   5. + SAMuon-lite bulk gain (A2); + scaled WD (A5) or Hyperball (A6), not both
   6. + AF-Muon for the tied table (A4); + ORCA early orthogonal reg (A3)
   7. FFN width schedule front-loaded (F1)
   8. ARCHITECTURE BAKE-OFF at equal FLOPs: plain vs Hyperloop [begin|mid x r|end] (B2, with
      B4 orthogonal injection, C4 symmetric-break stream init, G1 depth-aware init) vs
      Attractor (E1). Optionally + LPT shared KV (B3) or LoopMTP (C6).
   9. Data-side extras: stack-task warm-up 1% (E3); weak-teacher KD (F3); MIR for multi-epoch
      (A8)
Planner rules to add now: Step-Law-small LR/batch formulas (D1), WD ~ sqrt(overtraining)
(A1), phi-based looped quality estimate (B1), batch-size caveat for optimizer priors (A9),
deep-thin shapes at 50-150M (D2), no n-gram memory with repeated data (D4), and penalize
GQA+SWA+short ctx for the long-context purpose (E4).

Hardware notes: everything recommended is pure PyTorch + SDPA, with no flash-attn/Triton
required. On T4 (fp16): keep Muon Newton-Schulz and any fixed-point solver in fp32. The sink
logit needs a float attn_mask, so SDPA uses the mem-efficient kernel (fine on both GPUs).
Gated-DeltaNet hybrids (F2) are deferred until WSL2/Triton is confirmed.

=====================================================================================
SECTION A - OPTIMIZERS, WEIGHT DECAY, LR SCHEDULES
=====================================================================================
Context: Muon (Newton-Schulz orthogonalized momentum on 2-D hidden matrices; AdamW for
embeddings/head/norms) is the established baseline upgrade. On T4 run Newton-Schulz in fp32
(fp16 NS can overflow); at <=300M params the fp32 NS matmuls are cheap relative to fwd/bwd.
The 2026 literature is now about *improving on Muon*. Many variants exist; most are tested at
>=1B or give ~0.1% gains. The ones below survived because they were validated at 51M-253M or
are nearly free to implement.

A1. [ADOPT] Optimizer Memory Schedules for Outscaling the Overtraining Axis
    arXiv 2609.04577 (2026-09-04) - Everett, Qiu - https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.04577
    What: AdamW vs Muon vs SOAP vs ADANA at exactly our scale (51M-253M params), with the LR
      swept at every point, across overtraining factors from 1x to 256x Chinchilla.
    Findings that matter to us:
      - Muon and SOAP give a roughly CONSTANT token-efficiency gain over AdamW across horizons.
        This makes Muon a safe default and gives the planner a fixed "effective compute"
        multiplier for it.
      - The best weight decay scales roughly as sqrt(overtraining factor). Small models on
        a consumer GPU are usually overtrained (many tokens per param), so WD should rise with
        the token budget.
      - The best LR schedule can REVERSE between short and long horizons. Don't hard-code one.
      - ADANA (momentum-scheduled AdamW) with log-time WD + momentum cooldown overtakes Muon at
        very long horizons. Note it as a future option for long Colab runs.
    For Quark: planner rules (WD = base * sqrt(tokens / (20*N))) plus the Muon prior multiplier.
    Cheap, high value. No code module needed beyond a WD rule.

A2. [ADOPT] SAMuon / SAMuon-lite (Spectral-Aware Muon)
    arXiv 2608.25990 (2026-08-26) - Wu, Yu, Zhang, Woodland - https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.25990
    What: Spectral probing shows that a few top singular directions ("head", at edge of stability)
      need small steps, while the large "bulk" tolerates much bigger steps than Muon gives it.
      SAMuon-lite pins the top direction(s) at Muon scale (power iteration, rank 1) and amplifies
      the rest of the orthogonalized update by one scalar gain. Uses 30% "spectral warmup"
      for the gain.
    Evidence: modded-nanogpt 124M -> 1B. Needs 13-24% fewer tokens than Muon for the same
      val loss. Lite keeps most of that with ~zero wall-clock overhead. No extra optimizer state.
    Caveats: Tested at large batches (1024-4096 seqs). Our effective batch is much smaller, and
      2610.08975 (A9) shows optimizer rankings can flip with batch size. Needs an ablation.
    For Quark: optim/muon.py gets a `bulk_gain` option (1.0 = plain Muon). It's ~20 lines.
      Strong candidate.

A3. [TRY] ORCA - Orthogonal Regularization, Cooled After
    arXiv 2610.06116 (2026-10-05) - Liu, Jiang, Hou, Tao, et al. - https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.06116
    What: Adds a strong soft-orthogonality penalty on weight matrices during an early window of
      training, then switches it off abruptly at one step. Used with Muon (8 NS iterations, Polar
      Express coefficients). For MoE they release the layers in a staggered way (first layer at
      20%, last at 60%).
    Evidence: LLaMA/Qwen3 from 130M to 8B. Claims ORCA's gain over Muon >= Muon's gain over Adam,
      and the 130M LLaMA is one of the tested sizes. Minimal overhead.
    Caveats: Brand new (5 days old) and unreplicated. Licensed CC BY-NC-SA. Two new hyperparameters
      (strength, window). Could stack with SAMuon, but this is untested.
    For Quark: a "trick" toggle that adds a penalty for steps < release_step. Cheap ablation.

A4. [TRY] AF-Muon - AdamW-free Muon for tied-embedding models
    arXiv 2610.01395 (2026-10-01) - Lagzian et al. - https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.01395
    What: Small models almost always TIE embeddings, and the vocab table is a big share of the
      params (e.g. 50k x 512 = 25M). AF-Muon replaces the AdamW fallback for the tied table with a
      "support-aware finite-cap" LMO update, and uses RMS-normalized updates for 1-D params.
      This removes all second-moment state.
    Evidence: decoder LMs 124M-1B plus other modalities. Beats Hybrid Muon (Muon + AdamW) on val
      loss. ~20% less optimizer memory, ~1% step overhead. Accepted at the NeurIPS OPT workshop.
    For Quark: helps both quality and the 8 GB budget. Pair with weight tying. Keep Muon+AdamW
      as the fallback.
    Related, same idea for UNtied I/O layers: MuonIO 2610.02705 (column-norm embedding, row-norm
      head; tested at 1B). [NOTE] Use it only if tying is off.

A5. [TRY] Scaled weight decay (Muon-SW): WD_t = WD * lr_t / lr_max
    arXiv 2607.23777 (2026-07-26) - Apte - https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.23777
    What: One line. WD decays together with the LR schedule, so the weight norm settles instead of
      steadily shrinking. Reached the same loss ~30% faster at the largest scale (72M-930M,
      ~600 tokens/param, MoE models).
    Caveats: Shown on MoE only, single author. 600 tok/param is close to our regime, though.
    For Quark: a `wd_schedule: constant|lr_scaled` option. Trivial to ablate.

A6. [TRY] Hyperball (Fantastic Pretraining Optimizers II) + its critique
    arXiv 2606.16899 (2026-06-15) - Wen, Dang, Lyu, Ma, et al. - https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.16899
    Critique: "Hyperball May Not Be a Free Lunch" arXiv 2607.22444 (code on GitHub)
    Also: "Effective Learning Rate Governs Loss Dynamics" arXiv 2608.24814
    What: Replaces weight decay. Fix each weight matrix's Frobenius norm AND the update's norm
      (project back to the sphere after each step), so the angular LR is controlled directly.
      Muon-Hyperball: 20-30% token-equivalent speedup vs weight decay (Qwen3-style, up to 1.2B).
      Better LR transfer across widths and depths. That's useful for us: tune on a tiny model and
      transfer the settings.
    Critique: The gain comes from the effective-step-size schedule, not a better direction. MuonH
      starts slower and overtakes later, and aggressive LR decay can hurt it. ELR paper: WD and
      Hyperball both act only through effective LR = LR / ||W||.
    For Quark: a `norm_control: weight_decay|hyperball` slot. It's an ALTERNATIVE to A5 (scaled
      WD), not a complement, and needs its own LR schedule. Overlaps with A1's WD rule.

A7. [NOTE] WSqD - horizon-free LR schedule
    arXiv 2607.10959 (2026-07-12) - Ma, Chen - https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.10959
    What: WSD, but the stable phase is a shifted 1/sqrt(t) decay. Peak LR is horizon-independent,
      so you can EXTEND a run (resume from pre-decay) without the peak becoming wrong. Matches
      or beats tuned WSD on SlimPajama.
    For Quark: fits the Colab "keep training across sessions" workflow perfectly. Add it as a
      schedule option next to WSD. It costs ~10 lines.

A8. [NOTE] Data-Constrained Pretraining: Masked-Input Regularization (MIR) + SoftQ scaling law
    arXiv 2606.06888 (2026-06-05) - Xu, Wu, Cho, Hu, et al. - code: github.com/yixinw-lab/dc_pretrain
    What: For MULTI-EPOCH training on a finite dataset (common for us: TinyStories or a domain
      corpus), add an auxiliary next-token loss on a randomly masked copy of the input, on top of
      strong WD. It worked at 72M-1.4B and is worth ~1.3x more unique data. No inference cost.
      SoftQ is a scaling law that fits repeated-data runs better than Chinchilla.
    Cost: one extra forward/backward on the masked copy (~2x compute per step) or a split batch.
      Check the repo for how they budget it.
    For Quark: [TRY] when the planner detects > ~2 epochs. Use SoftQ in the planner's
      quality estimator when data is repeated.

A9. [NOTE] The Best Optimizer Depends on Batch Size
    arXiv 2610.08975 (2026-10-06) - Dang, Wen, Malladi
    No principled Muon batch-size scaling rule works consistently, and the winning optimizer
    changes with batch size. Our batches are small (memory-bound). Any "X beats Muon" claim made
    at 1M+ token batches needs re-checking at ours. This is a planner warning.

A10. [NOTE] PACE (pull live weights toward their EMA) / Terminal Shrinkage Averaging
    arXiv 2606.25086 (PACE, GPT-2 FineWeb pretraining gains) and 2609.25482 (TSA)
    Cheap at our scale: keep a weight EMA (memory = 1 extra copy, fine at <300M) and evaluate
    the EMA. PACE adds a clipped pull of the live weights toward the EMA. Low-risk add-on, but
    it overlaps with WSD cooldown gains. A later ablation.

Reviewed in this cluster and SKIPPED (one line each):
  - MeqMuon 2609.35701, DGA-Muon 2610.06578, NorMuon variants: row/col-equilibrated Muon,
    incremental over A2/A4. Revisit only if A2 fails.
  - BulkBoost 2610.07497: same insight as SAMuon, but tested only in continued pretraining, with
    ~0.1% loss gains. SAMuon (A2) is the from-scratch version.
  - Musec 2609.11655: a stability fix for Muon divergence. At our scale QK-norm + lower LR is
    enough. Keep it in mind only if fp16 Muon on T4 spikes.
  - AMUSE 2605.22432: schedule-free Muon. Interesting for "anytime" training, but WSqD/WSD cover
    it more simply.
  - LionMuon 2609.35297, Dion3 2608.11612, adaptive-NS 2609.33047: they cut Muon's NS/communication
    cost, which matters at scale and multi-GPU. NS isn't our bottleneck.
  - Clean 2610.04204, SoftServe, GO-Muon, Newton-Muon 2604.01472: second-order or extra state.
    Memory-heavy, small or unclear gains for us.
  - Spectrally Targeted Muon 2610.10965, Soren, COREM, Pion, RODE: analysis or unproven variants.
  - Why Adaptive Optimizers Underestimate Rare Tokens 2609.37535: theory. Muon preserves the mean
    output embedding and Adam doesn't. That supports A4 (Muon-style updates for the vocab
    table), but it isn't a module.

SECTION B - LOOPED / RECURRENT-DEPTH (the main "fewer params, same quality" lever)
=====================================================================================
Big picture first. A looped model reuses a block r times, so the params drop but the training
FLOPs per token stay those of the UNROLLED depth. The question for us is whether looping beats
a non-looped model at the same COMPUTE with fewer PARAMS, which is the project's stated ideal.
As of 2026-10 the answer is yes, but only with the right design (begin/middle/end split +
hyper-connections or shared memory). Naive looping loses at equal compute.

B1. [NOTE - core planner math] How Much Is One Recurrence Worth? Iso-Depth Scaling Laws
    arXiv 2604.21106 (2026-04-22, v3) - Schwethelm, Rueckert, Kaissis - https://arxiv.org/abs/2604.21106
    Fits L = E + A*(N_once + r^phi * N_rec)^-alpha + B*D^-beta. Finds phi = 0.46. Looping a block
      4x is worth about 4^0.46 = 1.9 unique copies, not 4. Example: at r=4 a 410M looped model
      ~ a 580M plain model, but it costs the training compute of a 1B plain model.
    Hyper-connections raise phi to 0.65 (a real capacity gain). Truncated BPTT LOWERS phi to 0.38
      (it trains the loop badly, even though val loss looks fine).
    For Quark: drop this formula straight into the planner's quality estimator for looped
      configs. It is exactly what's needed to rank "looped, fewer params" against "plain, more
      params" honestly. Also a rule: no truncated BPTT by default.

B2. [ADOPT] Hyperloop Transformers (looped middle block + hyper-connections between loops)
    arXiv 2604.21254 (2026-04-23, v3 Jul) - Zeitoun, Torroba-Hennigen, Yoon Kim (MIT) - CC BY 4.0
    What: Layers split [begin | middle x r | end], e.g. [2L | 4L x3 | 2L] = 16 unrolled layers
      from 8 unique ones. Only the middle block loops. Hyper-connections (n parallel residual
      streams with small learned mixing) are applied only BETWEEN loops. That adds just 150-300K
      params and ~2-5% throughput cost (mHC on every layer costs ~35%).
    Results (FineWeb-Edu, AdamW + cosine, the SAME unrolled depth, so the same train FLOPs/token):
      d=1024 depth 16, 12.5B tok: Transformer ppl 14.65 | Looped 14.85 | Hyperloop 14.40
      d=2048 depth 18, 50B tok:   Transformer 10.19   | Looped 10.02 | Hyperloop  9.65
      d=2048 depth 38, 100B tok:  Transformer  8.60   | Looped  8.68 | Hyperloop  8.49
      Hyperloop uses ~50% of the baseline's params and also leads on downstream accuracy.
      INT4 GPTQ quantizes well too.
    HONEST CAVEAT (their App. C): with a better-tuned init/LR for all models at the smallest
      size (240M baseline vs 136M Hyperloop), Hyperloop only MATCHES the Transformer
      (13.08 vs 12.94 ppl) while mHC is best. Even so, matching with 57% of the params is the
      project goal.
    Fit for us: no special kernels; it's plain PyTorch. Fewer params means a smaller
      optimizer state, which is a real win on 8 GB. Activation memory is unchanged (same
      unrolled depth). Works with SDPA, fp16, and Muon.
    For Quark: block/hyperloop.py, with params (n_begin, n_mid, r, n_end, n_streams=4).
      This is the top candidate for the "small but punches above its size" model.

B3. [TRY] Looped Prediction Transformer (LPT) - shared KV memory across recursions
    arXiv 2610.02383 (2026-10-01) - Monea, Ramji, El-Kurdi, Lastras, et al. (IBM)
    What: Only the 1st recursion writes a full KV cache. Later recursions attend to that shared
      cache plus a short local window of their own. Unexpectedly this IMPROVES quality: the
      shared memory acts as a gradient highway to recursion 1. The hybrid variant, with 5
      recursions, has 1.12-1.82 lower val ppl than a same-size (param-matched) Transformer on
      FineWeb-Edu at 150M-1B, plus 76-79% less KV memory.
    Caveat: the comparison is param-matched (looped uses more compute). Needs custom attention
      masking (the shared cache plus a local window). That works with SDPA by passing the
      recursion-1 K/V and a windowed mask, so there's no special kernel.
    For Quark: an option inside the looped block, `kv_sharing: none|first_recursion`. It
      stacks naturally with B2 (Hyperloop's middle block). Ablate B2 vs B2+B3.

B4. [TRY] Towards Looped Models Done Right, Part II: Rethinking at Fixed Points
    arXiv 2610.06833 (2026-10-05) - Huang, Shi, Chen, Wen, et al. - code: github.com/ifm-ai/xllm-loop
    Two cheap training-side upgrades, validated 100M-1.6B, that lower ppl at every scale:
      (a) ORTHOGONAL INPUT INJECTION: when re-injecting the input embedding into each loop,
          remove the component of the state that lies along the input direction, so the
          injection can't be amplified or cancelled.
      (b) a LEARNED depth prior (random loop counts during training, learned from prediction
          feedback with an entropy term) instead of Huginn's fixed broad prior. This enables
          terminal KV sharing at decode time.
    For Quark: (a) is a few lines and applies to any looped block; implement it with B2.
      (b) is only needed if we want variable test-time depth. Defer it.

B5. [NOTE] What Makes Recurrence Effective in Looped LMs?
    arXiv 2609.36636 (2026-09-29) - Zhuang, Wang, Razzak, Weiyang Liu
    Design guidance: keep NON-recurrent output layers (that matches Hyperloop's "end" block).
      Recurrence helps reasoning but can hurt knowledge/fact recall. Channel-wise
      "history-state injection" + timestep (loop-index) conditioning beats plain initial-state
      injection. Use it as an alternative to B4(a) when ablating.

B6. [NOTE] Closing the Loop: Practical Training Recipes for Looped LMs
    arXiv 2610.00673 (2026-09-30) - Marchenko et al.
    A 1.4B LoopLM beats a param-matched dense model on all 12 benchmarks with the same data
      (+14 GSM8K, +22 DROP). At matched inference compute it approaches a 3.9B dense model with
      36% of the params. Recipe: LR warmup + stronger exit-gate regularization makes from-scratch
      looped training stable (no multi-stage schedule). It also gives a minimal dense->looped
      conversion (one learned input-mixing scalar + smoothed exit loss). That's useful later:
      train a plain model, then convert it.

B7. [NOTE - honest negative at OUR scale] CART: Context-Anchored Recurrent Transformer
    arXiv 2606.01495 (2026-05-31) - Capps - code + results DB: github.com/ccapps42/CART
    Trained on SINGLE CONSUMER GPUs, d=256-1024, ~1B tokens, 3 seeds. At the d=1024 param-parity
      test it does NOT beat a dense baseline (-1-2% at stored-param parity, ~-10% at
      effective-param parity). Its fancy recurrent-core parts (hyper-connections, LTI gate,
      loop-index embedding) were individually vestigial. The prelude depth P mattered more than
      the loop count R.
    Lesson: at ~1B tokens and small width, looping's advantage is fragile. Hyperloop's
      begin/end blocks echo "prelude depth dominates". Our ablation must compare at equal
      compute AND equal params, and expect small effects.

Reviewed and SKIPPED (looped cluster):

  - FlashLoop 2609.29812, ResidualQuant 2610.10381, Loopy 2610.05265, WaveFront/LoopSpec/DLoop
    decoding: inference/quantization/serving for looped LMs. Not needed at our size.
  - Loop Dropout 2609.34218: LoRA fine-tuning regularizer.
  - InfiLoop 2610.11570: a 7M model for Sudoku/ARC with 20k loops. Puzzle-specific, not

  - Looped MoE papers (2609.35751, 2609.40316, 2610.01153): MoE on 8 GB at our scale is not

SECTION C - RESIDUAL STREAM, NORMALIZATION, EMBEDDINGS, MTP
=====================================================================================
C1. [ADOPT] LayerRoPE - depth-wise "RoPE" on the RMSNorm gains
    arXiv 2610.09179 (2026-10-06) - Srivastava, Kanan (U. Rochester) - CC BY 4.0
    Project page: www.cs.rochester.edu/u/ssrivas9/layer-rope
    What: Observes that per-layer norm gains (gamma) in trained LLMs grow and ROTATE with depth,
      acting as an implicit layer-index code. It makes this explicit: ONE shared gamma vector per
      norm site (pre-attn, pre-MLP) for ALL layers. Layer l's gain = shared gamma, with pairs
      treated as complex numbers, times a depth-conditioned magnitude (log-linear in l) and a
      rotation (per-layer base angle spread over pairs with a RoPE-like frequency spectrum,
      base = hyperparameter). It uses ~4 scalars per site. No matmuls, ~0 FLOPs, and it has
      FEWER params than per-layer gammas.
    Evidence (LLaMA-style on C4, Chinchilla budget, LR tuned PER METHOD at every scale, 58M-1.3B):
      - 134M ablation, Pre-Norm: loss 3.1003 -> 3.0317 (ppl 22.2 -> 20.7). Peri-Norm:
        3.072 -> 3.025. Magnitude alone gives most of it, and magnitude + rotation is best.
      - Reaches Pre-Norm's 1.3B loss with 3.4x less compute. Has a 3-10x wider LR basin
        (great for us: fewer LR sweeps). Stable to 512 layers. It TRANSFERS TO LOOPED models
        (Sec 4.4), so it can be combined with B2 Hyperloop.
    Caveats: 3 days old and unreplicated. The LR sweeps are a strong point in its favor.
    For Quark: norm/layerrope.py as a toggle on top of RMSNorm pre-norm. Implementation is
      ~40 lines, and the depth index is just the layer number (for looped blocks, use the
      unrolled index or loop index; check their App. D.4). This is the best effort-to-gain
      ratio in this sweep. Ablate it first.

C2. [ADOPT] Value residual (static), with SATFormer as the [TRY] upgrade
    SATFormer: "Transformers with Selective Access to Early Representations"
    arXiv 2605.03953 (2026-05-05) - Gunasekaran, Wright, Rui-Jie Zhu, Eshraghian
    code: github.com/SkyeGunasekaran/SATFormer
    What: The value residual (mix layer-1's V into every later layer's V with learned
      coefficients, as in ResFormer/modded-nanogpt) is the cheapest "early representation"
      shortcut. SATFormer replaces the static mixing coefficient with a context-dependent gate
      (per token, per head), at ~baseline throughput and memory.
    Evidence: 130M-1.3B. Beats both the static value residual and the plain Transformer on val
      loss and zero-shot accuracy. About +1.5 points over the value residual on retrieval-heavy
      tasks.
    Independent support for the static value residual at our scale: JugnuLM 2609.14715 (Section
      D) kept value residuals + Muon at 110M (ARC-Easy +3.6 combined).
    For Quark: attention option `value_residual: off|static|gated`. Memory cost: hold V_1 for
      the whole forward pass (one [B,T,d] tensor), which is negligible.

C3. [TRY] NAG - Norm-Agnostic residual networks (+ built-in Mixture-of-Depths)
    arXiv 2606.16112 (2026-06-15) - Figliolia, Millidge (Zyphra)
    What: Separates magnitude from direction in the residual stream so later layers aren't
      drowned by residual-norm growth. Gains grow with depth, so it suits deep-thin small
      models (the JugnuLM-style 23x576 geometry). Its MoD variant skips ~20-25% of layer calls
      while matching full-depth loss at equal training compute.
    Overlap: C1 (LayerRoPE) tackles the same "curse of depth" from the gain side. Pick one and
      ablate the other. LayerRoPE has stronger small-scale numbers on paper.
    For Quark: [TRY] after C1. MoD skipping makes variable shapes, which is awkward without
      torch.compile on Windows, so use the dense version first.

C4. [NOTE] Hyper-connection practicalities (needed if B2 Hyperloop is adopted)
    - Stream collapse: 2606.03483 (Alimaskina et al.). HC/mHC streams often collapse into one
      dominant stream, and mixing stays near identity. Fix: BREAK SYMMETRY at stream init
      (different init per stream). That improves all mHC variants. It's a cheap init detail to
      bake into block/hyperloop.py.
    - xHC 2607.14530: n=16 streams with sparse updates. Shown on 18B/28B MoE, and memory traffic
      is the bottleneck. [SKIP] for us; n=4 is plenty.
    - Note: in Hyperloop's tuned-init table, full mHC (on every layer) was the best small model
      but cost ~35% throughput. On a fixed GPU-hour budget that's usually a loss.

C5. [SKIP for now] Kronecker Embeddings (byte-level factorized input embeddings)
    arXiv 2605.29459 (2026-05-28) - Shravan - code: github.com/theschoolofai/kronecker-embeddings
    Claim: 124M nanoGPT, 2.5B tokens, 3 seeds: 2.5% lower val loss than BPE-tied, 1.43x fewer
      steps. The input table becomes a fixed byte codec + one projection.
    Why skip: at OUR scale it does NOT save params. Weight tying becomes impossible, so the output
      head returns as a separate V x d matrix. The paper admits it has no UNTIED-BPE baseline,
      so the gain may come from untying rather than from Kronecker. Revisit if someone runs that
      control. (A cheap thing WE can test: tied vs untied at our scale.)

C6. MTP cluster (multi-token prediction)
    General fact restated in LightMTP: token-level MTP HURTS models below ~3B (Gloeckle 2024).
    - LightMTP 2610.06031 [NOTE]: latent MTP that bootstraps targets from the model's own hidden
      states, adds <=1% params, and the head is discarded at inference. It still has a (smaller)
      next-token cost, with gains on planning (Countdown) and code. Up to 1.5B. Consider it only
      for a "reasoning/planning" purpose profile, not for raw LM loss.
    - LoopMTP 2608.03624 [TRY, only with looped models]: aligns loop t's hidden state with
      the embedding of the token t steps ahead, plus a gate. Up to +8.1% relative accuracy vs
      non-looped, and stable to 15 loops. It gives the loop iterations a supervision signal at
      almost no cost. Pair it with B2.
    - AdaMTP 2608.00434, HiLP 2608.05806, inference-MTP/speculative papers: [SKIP]
      (7-12B fine-tuning, or decoding speed only).

C7. [SKIP] Sphere Retraction Normalizations (GeoNorm family) 2608.02668
    Spherical residual stream. Beats "lightweight deep connection schemes" on nanoGPT, but there
    are no strong baselines or numbers at our scale, and it overlaps C1/C3. Not worth it ahead
    of LayerRoPE.

SECTION D - SMALL-SCALE RECIPES & PLANNER CALIBRATION (directly at our size)

D1. [ADOPT - planner] Step Law recalibrated below 59M params
    arXiv 2609.27581 (2026-09-23) - Romanyukov et al.
    935 runs, nanoGPT + TinyStories, 2048-token BPE vocab, AdamW, warmup-cosine, 4 <= D/N <= 600.
      eta*(N, D) = 0.0985 * N^-0.508 * D^0.238
      B*(D)      = 3.6e-4 * D^0.931   (batch in tokens; independent of N)
    Original Step Law OVERESTIMATES the optimal LR by ~4x (2.4x-6.6x) at this size. For Quark
      these formulas go straight into the planner as AdamW defaults for N < 60M (use the
      original Step Law above that). Muon needs its own LR rule (see A1/A9).

D2. [NOTE] JugnuLM-53M vs 110M: a sub-150M ablation ladder
    arXiv 2609.14715 (2026-09-13) - Rajput, Chauhan, Kosaraju
    Qwen3-style (GQA, RoPE, SwiGLU, RMSNorm, QK-Norm, z-loss), FineWeb-Edu. DEEP-AND-THIN
      23 layers x 576 hidden at 110M matched GPT-class 125M models on BLiMP with ~12% fewer
      params and fewer tokens (8B). Kept: value residuals + Muon (+3.6 ARC-Easy). Rejected:
      a diverse data blend, and logit distillation from a 1.7B teacher (perplexity cost).
    For Quark: the planner's shape grid should favor deep-thin (aspect ratio d/L ~ 25) at
      50-150M. The baseline component list is validated here.

D3. [NOTE] L20-Edu-135M: full single-GPU 135M pipeline
    arXiv 2606.22189 (2026-06-20) - Yin Li - HF: AliceYin/l20-edu-135m
    134.5M on ONE L20 GPU, 13B tokens (10B FineWeb-Edu + 3B edu/math/code mix). Reaches 87% of
      SmolLM-135M's zero-shot mean with 2.2% of its tokens. Documents MinHash dedup,
      benchmark-overlap removal, and throughput tuning. GRPO RLVR at 135M made GSM8K WORSE.
    For Quark: a realistic quality target for our hardware, and data hygiene steps for
      quark data prepare. Don't bother with RL at this size.

D4. [NOTE - warning] Forking: sudden overfitting under data replay
    arXiv 2610.00394 (2026-09-30) - Yu, Guo, et al. - code on GitHub
    Models with an n-gram memory branch (Engram-style hashed n-gram embeddings, a trick that
      autoresearch agents in modded-nanogpt land like to add) show a sharp train/val split at
      EPOCH BOUNDARIES when data is repeated. Low-frequency contexts drive it.
    For Quark: if we ever add n-gram/Engram embeddings, the planner must forbid them with
      multi-epoch data, and the trainer's gen-gap monitor should check at epoch boundaries.

SECTION E - ATTENTION VARIANTS, FIXED-POINT MODELS, DATA-SIDE TRICKS
=====================================================================================
E1. [TRY - high upside] Attractor Models ("Solve the Loop")
    arXiv 2605.12466 (2026-05-12) - Fein-Ashley, Rashidinejad (USC)
    code: github.com/jacobfa/Attractor - site: attractor-models.github.io - CC BY 4.0
    What: Standard Transformer backbone proposes output embeddings. Then an "attractor" block is
      iterated to its FIXED POINT (Anderson acceleration, or just a few plain iterations), with
      gradients from implicit differentiation using a ONE-STEP backward approximation. So
      training MEMORY is constant in effective depth: there's no backprop through every loop,
      which is ideal for 8 GB. The model learns to put its initial proposal near the
      equilibrium ("equilibrium internalization"), so the solver can be DROPPED at inference
      with little loss.
    Evidence (nanochat recipe, FineWeb-Edu, PARAM-matched, same data/optimizer/schedule):
      140M: Transformer val ppl 21.48 | Parcae (looped) 19.06 | Attractor 18.30 (-14.8%);
            Lambada ppl 127 -> 68. Gains hold at 370M and 770M. The 770M model ~ a 1.3B
            Transformer trained on 2x tokens. Ablations are at 60M / 1B tokens (our regime).
            One-step backward ~ Anderson quality and is much cheaper.
    Caveats: Param-matched, NOT compute-matched; the forward solver iterations add FLOPs that
      I couldn't find quantified in the text, so we must MEASURE tokens/sec. Only one group so far.
      Implicit-diff models can be finicky in fp16 (T4): keep the solver in fp32 or bf16 on the
      3070 Ti.
    How it relates to Section B: it's an alternative to explicit looping (B2 Hyperloop),
      because it buys depth-like refinement without the r-times activation memory. Both
      directly serve the "fewer params" goal. Ablate B2 vs E1 head to head at ~60M.
    For Quark: block/attractor.py (backbone + fixed-point block + one-step grad). Start from
      their repo.

E2. [TRY - cheap] Sink logit + value gate ("Abstention and Noise Filtering")
    arXiv 2609.22005 (2026-09-18, v2) - Richard Zhe Wang
    What: Two missing softmax primitives, each given its own mechanism:
      - ABSTENTION: a learnable per-head "sink logit" (a phantom key with a learned logit and a
        zero value, as in gpt-oss), so a head can output ~nothing.
      - FILTERING: a gate on each value read, either a norm gate or a projection gate
        sigmoid(linear(v)), i.e. value-state gated attention.
    Evidence: 10M/50M/124M/350M from scratch on FineWeb-Edu, 3 paired seeds (rigorous for this
      scale). Abstention gain: 0.0185 nats at 10M, shrinking to ~0.005 at 124M. Filtering gain:
      0 at 10M, rising to ~0.009 at 350M. The two are mostly additive, so sink+proj is best.
      Small but nearly free, and it removes attention sinks (helps fp16 stability and
      quantization).
    SDPA note: the sink logit = append one extra key column through a float attn_mask
      (per-head bias), with a zero value row. Works with the mem-efficient kernel on T4.
      The flash kernel doesn't accept arbitrary masks, so on the 3070 Ti the mem-efficient
      kernel would be used (a small speed hit).
    For Quark: attention toggles `sink_logit` and `value_gate: none|norm|proj`. At <=50M prefer
      sink_logit alone. Above ~100M add proj.

E3. [TRY - data] Abstract "stack" warm-up before natural-language pretraining
    arXiv 2609.38764 (2026-09-30) - Shinnick, Saratchandran, Teney, van den Hengel
    Project: zlshinnick.github.io/beyond-perplexity
    What: Spend ~1% of the token budget FIRST on an algorithmically generated stack-manipulation
      task, then train normally. In SMALL LMs this improves multi-hop QA (+3.9 F1 MuSiQue,
      plus HotpotQA and 2Wiki) at equal perplexity. The structure matters (stack works, queue
      doesn't). The timing matters (it must be a dedicated initial phase; mixing it in or
      adding it later doesn't work). The benefit persists through billions of tokens.
    For Quark: a `data.warmup: stack_task(1%)` option. Generator is trivial and costs nothing
      in memory. Note: loss curves won't show the benefit, so it only shows up in evals.

E4. [NOTE] Cracks in the Foundation (COLM 2026) 2608.10296 - Bertsch, Soldaini, Neubig, et al.
    Four "minor" choices (normalization type, GQA, short pretraining context, sliding window)
    each slightly hurt long-context EXTENSION and compound (up to -47% downstream when 3+ are
    combined). The damage is invisible in short-context val loss. For Quark: a planner soft rule
    for the long-context purpose profile. Penalize stacking GQA + SWA + short train ctx when
    YaRN/DaRoPE extension is planned.

E5. [NOTE] Selecting Repetition Counts in Data-Constrained Pretraining 2610.05126
    The best number of epochs over a target corpus changes with model size (16 -> 8 repeats
    was better at 520M). Use small proxy runs to shortlist epoch counts. Fits the planner's
    multi-epoch warning (pair with A8 MIR).

Reviewed and SKIPPED:
  - Parallax 2605.29157 (local-linear attention): gains at 0.6B/1.7B, and it pairs well with
    Muon, but it needs a custom kernel to be efficient. A non-starter on T4 and Windows for now.
  - GAPE 2605.10414 (gated adaptive PE): long-context extrapolation. DaRoPE already covers
    that need in this project.
  - Attention sinks unified view 2606.08105: analysis on vision transformers. E2 is the
    actionable version.
  - Self-Pruned KV attention 2605.14037, BF1 sparse attention, Nexusformer, TinyCeNN: KV/serving
    or model-growth topics, not small-model quality.
  - Small LLMs: Pruning vs. Training from Scratch 2606.14150: prunes Llama-3.1-8B, which
    needs hardware we don't have.

SECTION F - HYBRIDS, FFN ALLOCATION, TOKENIZATION, DISTILLATION, MULTI-EPOCH

F1. [TRY - free param reallocation] Geometry-Guided Layerwise FFN Width Allocation
    arXiv 2608.02064 (2026-08-03) - Mudarisov, Burtsev, State
    What: Instead of a constant FFN width, allocate the SAME total FFN params unevenly across
      depth. The geometry-based ("normalized work") schedules are FRONT-LOADED (wider FFNs in
      early layers). In PAIRED 128M and 256M training runs, they lower mean val loss vs uniform
      AND vs a hand-made cosine taper. The gap grows at 440M.
    Cost: zero at train time (same params and FLOPs). The schedule can be measured once on a
      small pilot model, or just use a fixed front-loaded taper as the default.
    For Quark: a planner/shape option `ffn_width_schedule: uniform|front_loaded|measured`.
      Easy ablation. Note the interaction with B2: in a looped model the shared middle block
      has one width, so allocation applies to the begin/end blocks.

F2. [NOTE - later, toolchain permitting] Hybrid linear-attention models
    - Olmo Hybrid 2604.03444 (AI2, 7B): replacing sliding-window layers with Gated DeltaNet
      layers beat Olmo 3 and SCALES MORE EFFICIENTLY than the transformer. That's the strongest
      controlled evidence so far that hybrids are better per FLOP in pretraining, not just at
      inference.
    - Head-wise Hybrid (HwH) 2609.02986: NoPE full-attention heads for global retrieval plus
      linear-attention heads for local/positional work, at FA:LA < 1:3. Better retrieval and
      length extrapolation. It conceptually overlaps with DaRoPE (slow RoPE bands are the
      problem).
    Why not now: Gated DeltaNet needs Triton kernels (flash-linear-attention). Those are painful
      on native Windows and fp16/T4 support is uncertain, and a pure-PyTorch recurrence is too
      slow. Revisit if we move local training to WSL2 and confirm fla works on T4.
    Skipped: TALH small MLA-SSM hybrid 2609.29618 (single seed, unmatched params, author says
      no general conclusions).

F3. [TRY - distillation that fits our hardware] Strong Teacher Not Needed? Distillation in LLM pretraining
    arXiv 2605.23857 (2026-05-22) - Taiming Lu, Zhuang Liu
    Finding: with the right mix of LM loss + KD loss, even SMALL, UNDERTRAINED teachers improve
      students (including larger ones). Stronger teachers can saturate or REVERSE the gains. KD
      helps generalization (OOD + downstream) more than in-domain loss.
    Why it matters: our "needs a huge teacher" kill criterion doesn't apply. A same-tokenizer
      ~100-350M open model (e.g. SmolLM2-135M/360M with its 49k tokenizer) can run as a teacher
      in fp16 next to the student on 8 GB. Or cache the teacher's top-k logits to disk once
      and reuse them across epochs.
    Caveats: no HTML full text, so I couldn't verify sizes or the mixing ratio. JugnuLM (D2)
      found KD from a 1.7B teacher raised perplexity, consistent with "stronger isn't better".
    Also: "Divergence controls entropy in distillation" 2610.03529. Forward KL inflates student
      entropy; reverse KL deflates it. Use forward KL + CE mixing (the standard) and watch
      sample sharpness.
    For Quark: train/kd.py with options (teacher path, alpha, top-k cache). The tokenizer must
      match the teacher's.

F4. [NOTE] q0: Primitives for Hyper-Epoch Pretraining 2606.03938
    For heavily repeated small datasets: a cyclic LR with anti-correlated WD collects diverse
    snapshots, "chain distillation" trains each snapshot against its predecessor, and a learned
    ensemble prior combines them. 1.8B on 100M tokens matched a 256-epoch ensemble in ~56
    epochs. It yields an ENSEMBLE (more inference cost). Chain distillation alone is the piece
    worth borrowing for TinyStories-style many-epoch runs.

F5. [NOTE] Looped GPT-BERT (BabyLM 2026 strict-small) 2609.09691
    A 12.18M model (4 physical layers x 12 loops) on 7.5M words matches BabyLM GPT-2/GPT-BERT
    10M-word baselines on BLiMP/GLUE with fewer params. This is more evidence that looping pays
    off when DATA is the limit. Too few physical layers limits some tasks. The planner should
    favor looping for small/repeated datasets.

F6. [NOTE] Tokenization
    - CoBPE 2610.05597 (COLM 2026): compositional tokens (base word + surface modifiers),
      sequences 30% shorter, +1.2 downstream at matched compute, tested at 780M/1.3B. It's a
      new tokenizer plus a joint output head, a big build. Revisit after core components.
    - Byte-level simulation 2604.27263: subword wins mostly through THROUGHPUT (more text per
      step) plus boundary priors. Supports the plan's advice to use a small custom BPE (4-8k) for
      tiny models: still subword, smaller embedding/logit cost.

F7. [NOTE] GQA-muP 2605.15290: muP / Complete-P scalings derived for GQA, so LR transfers
    across GQA group counts and depth/WD settings. Use it if the planner does muP-style transfer
    from a tiny tuning proxy. Overlaps with Hyperball's (A6) LR-transfer benefit.

Reviewed and SKIPPED:
  - Wiola 2607.01394: "novel" 5-component SLM architecture with no training results (only
    "unit tests pass"). No evidence.
  - KANs for SLMs 2607.15525: the authors' own results show no consistent gain over SwiGLU.
  - OASIS 2604.09406: activation-subspace memory saving (2x peak memory). Gradient
    checkpointing + chunked CE is simpler for our model sizes.
  - Complexity-guided init 2607.09204: a negative result (spectral-matched init didn't help).

SECTION G - REMAINING NOTES & SKIPS

G1. [NOTE - needed if B2 is used] DeepLoop: Depth Scaling for Looped Transformers 2607.13491
    (Li, Zhang, Guo, Quanquan Gu). Residual scaling for looped models must count parameter
    VISITS, not physical layers: DeepNorm-style alpha = (2N)^1/2, beta = (8N)^-1/2 with N =
    unrolled depth. Neutral without loops, better val loss with loops (GPT-2 small/medium).
    It's Post-LN. For our Pre-Norm + LayerRoPE stack, the takeaway is to init/scale the shared
    middle block by UNROLLED depth (e.g. GPT-2-style out-proj init std / sqrt(2*N_unrolled)).

G2. [NOTE - capacity add-on, conflicts with the "small model" goal] Engram / n-gram memory
    FactorEngram 2609.35578 (340M/1B gains), Tensorized Engram 2606.08347 (fewer params than
    Engram), Memory Grafting 2605.20948 (offline teacher hidden states as n-gram memory).
    Lookup tables add params cheaply in FLOPs and could even live in CPU RAM, but they make the
    model BIGGER on disk, and Forking (D4) shows they overfit sharply under repeated data. Skip
    unless the purpose is "max quality, size doesn't matter".

G3. [SKIP] Do Transformers Need Three Projections? (ICML 2026) 2606.04032
    Shared K=V cuts the KV cache by 50% but costs ~3.1% perplexity at 300M/1.2B. That's an
    inference-memory trade, and we want quality per param.

G4. [SKIP] Fixed (non-trainable) input token codes 2610.04002 / 2605.09751: viable, but worse
    than a learned table (single runs, 1.7B). Same untied-head problem as Kronecker (C5).

G5. [SKIP] X-MoD 2609.34212 (sparse-depth routing): grows total params at fixed active compute,
    the opposite of our goal. LEAP / early-exit pretraining: inference speed.
