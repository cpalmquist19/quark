ML PAPERS 2 - arXiv sweep, 2026-09-18 to 2026-10-09
====================================================

Companion to ml_papers.txt. Same target as the DaRoPE report: Quark, 10-100M param decoder-only LMs,
context 512-2048, RTX 3070 Ti 8 GB (bf16, native Windows, SDPA only) and Colab T4 15 GB (fp16 +
GradScaler, no bf16, no FlashAttention), no custom CUDA.

How this list was made
- Queried the arXiv API for submissions in the window across: optimizers (Muon etc.), LR schedules,
  looped/recurrent depth, MTP, positional encodings, attention variants, stability/low precision,
  small-model pretraining, scaling laws/data repetition, hyperparameter transfer, memory-efficient training.
- Read the HTML full text of 26 candidates (method, model sizes, token budgets, hardware, seeds, numbers).
- 10 kept (below). 16 were killed or not selected, each with the reason (last section).
- Caveat on the reading: each page was read through a summarizer, and most pages were cut off at
  ~100k characters, so appendices (hardware, hyperparameters, code links) were often not seen.
  "Code not confirmed" below means "no link seen", not "no code exists". Numbers are as reported by
  the authors; almost all are single-paper, few-seed, and none are from our hardware.
- VERIFIED 2026-10-09: all 10 kept papers re-checked against the arXiv API (title/authors/dates) and
  the FULL HTML text (tables included, no summarizer). All exist and the core claims hold. Errors found
  were corrected in place and marked "[corrected]".
- Everything here is weaker evidence than a result on our own runs. Treat each as a candidate for the
  registry (maturity: promising/experimental), not as established.

Priority key: [A] build/adopt soon, [B] worth an ablation, [C] low-cost option / planner guidance.

A. ATTENTION
======================================================================

1. [A] Gated values + sink logit ("Abstention and Noise Filtering: Two Missing Primitives of Softmax Attention")
   arXiv 2609.22005 (v1 2026-09-18, v2 later) - Richard Zhe Wang - https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.22005
   What: two tiny additions to softmax attention.
     - sink logit: a learnable per-head scalar adds a phantom key (zero value) to the softmax denominator,
       so a head can "abstain".
     - value gate: a scalar in (0,1) per value read, sigmoid of a learned linear function of the value
       ("projection gate") or of its norm vs a learned threshold ("norm gate"), applied before mixing.
     - best variant: sink + projection gate. Extra params per layer: sink = H, proj gate = H(d+1),
       sink+proj = H(d+2) (H heads, d head dim). KV cache unchanged.
   Evidence at our scale: this is the closest scale match of any paper in this list.
     10M (6L, d384, ctx 512, 0.20B tok), 50M (10L, d640, ctx 1024, 0.95B tok), 124M, 350M.
     FineWeb-Edu, GPT-2 tokenizer, AdamW, bf16, ONE consumer GPU (RTX 5090), 3 paired seeds.
     Val-loss gain over baseline (nats), sink+proj: 0.022 (10M), 0.013 (50M), 0.011 (124M), 0.019 (350M).
     Sink alone: 0.019 / 0.007 / 0.005 / 0.010. Gains are small (~0.3-0.5% of loss) but consistent across
     tiers; zero-shot differences at 124M are within seed noise.
   Fit for Quark: attention-slot toggle ("attn:sink", "attn:vgate"). Cheap, no kernels.
     Value gate only needs V, so it can be applied before F.scaled_dot_product_attention (my inference;
     the paper does not describe this path). Sink can be done with an extra constant coordinate on q/k
     (paper pads head_dim to a multiple of 8 for fused kernels).
   Watch for:
     - fp16/T4 untested (gate math done in fp32 in the paper). Keep gates in fp32 on T4.
     - With RoPE/DaRoPE, add the phantom coordinate AFTER rotation (my inference - check).
     - Gate/sink params are tiny vectors: AdamW group, no weight decay on thresholds/sink (paper excludes
       the norm-gate threshold from decay). Keep out of Muon.
     - Norm gate got WORSE than baseline under heavy injected noise (at 124M already at dose 0.8:
       +4.40 vs +2.63 nats; and at 1.6) - prefer projection gate. [corrected]
     - 350M sink-logit seed spread is large; code release not confirmed.

2. [B] LayerRoPE (depth-wise rotary modulation of RMSNorm gains)
   arXiv 2610.09179 (2026-10-06) - Shikhar Srivastava, Christopher Kanan - https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.09179
   What: replace the per-layer RMSNorm weight vectors with ONE shared gain per norm site, multiplied by a
     depth-dependent complex factor c(l,j) = exp(r(l)) * exp(i*theta(l,j)), with r and theta functions of
     log(l+1) (4 sites: attn in/out, MLP in/out). About 4d+16 params instead of 2*L*d. No matmuls;
     paper estimates <0.02% training FLOPs overhead.
   Evidence: ladder 58M / 134M / 250M / 554M / 1.34B on C4 (seq len 256, LLaMA-2 tokenizer, ~4x Chinchilla).
     134M, 3 seeds at each method's tuned LR: 2.831 vs 2.915 for Pre-Norm (paired -0.085 +- 0.014;
     seed std <= 0.016; LayerRoPE lowest under every seed). (The often-quoted 3.0317 vs 3.1003 is a
     single-run ablation table, not the seeded result.) [corrected]
     Also tried inside a looped model (Parcae 140M): val PPL 18.66 -> 18.36. LR sensitivity reportedly
     3-10x lower. Optimal LR vs Pre-Norm: 4x (58M), 5x (368M), 2x (1.3B); vs Peri-Norm 1-2x. [corrected]
   Fit for Quark: new "norm" slot component ("norm:layerrope"). Elementwise only, SDPA-agnostic.
     Interesting for the looped block (Phase 5): gains are a function of depth, so loop iterations can
     get distinct gains for free.
   Watch for:
     - Only seq len 256 tested; nothing at 512-2048. Hardware not stated.
     - At 1B the authors had to move logits to fp32 under bf16 because output-logit scale grew.
       On the T4 (fp16, narrower range) keep logits fp32, monitor logit magnitude, keep z-loss available.
     - The paper is internally inconsistent about the scalar count (4 pairs vs 16 scalars) and init;
       implement from the equations, then unit-test that init reproduces plain RMSNorm.
     - Raised optimal LR means the planner must NOT reuse the RMSNorm LR prior.

3. [B] Towards Looped Models Done Right, Part II: Rethinking at Fixed Points
   arXiv 2610.06833 (2026-10-05) - Benhao Huang, Chufan Shi, Junlin Chen, et al.
   https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.06833   Code listed: github.com/ifm-ai/xllm-loop (contents not verified)
   What (for the Phase-5 looped block, prelude / recurrent core / coda):
     - Orthogonal input injection (OrthoInj): re-inject the prelude output q each iteration as
       zeta = Q_q * Lambda * h + q, with Q_q = I - q q^T / (||q||^2 + eps), i.e. remove from the carried
       state the component along the injected input. A few lines; no extra kernels.
     - Truncated BPTT through only the last 2 recurrences: memory/compute scale with the window, not the
       loop count. With b=2, extrapolating to more loops / terminal KV sharing cost <= 2% / 3% PPL
       (vs >25% / 21% with full BPTT at fixed R=5); b >= 4 matches full BPTT within 1% at 400M. [corrected]
     - Learned depth prior over training loop counts (policy-gradient style, +<=1.6% FLOPs).
   Evidence: 100M / 400M / 1.6B, 1 + 2R + 1 Llama-style layout. MixVal (held-out) PPL, learned prior vs
     fixed Poisson-lognormal prior: 5.67 vs 5.73 (100M), 3.93 vs 4.00 (400M); WikiText PPL 19.91 vs 20.19
     (100M), 12.28 vs 12.55 (400M). OrthoInj vs Parcae-decay (MixVal): 5.67 vs 5.75 (100M), 3.94 vs 3.96
     (400M). One seed per config. [corrected: the 5.67/5.73 numbers are MixVal, not WikiText]
   Fit for Quark: borrow OrthoInj + truncated BPTT as options of "block:looped". Skip the RL / distillation /
     KV-sharing parts (serving-side, or need large teachers).
   Watch for:
     - Smallest run is 100M on 21.5B tokens (~215 tok/param). That is ~15 days on a 3070 Ti by my
       estimate, so the RECIPE is out of budget; only the mechanisms transfer.
     - [corrected] There is NO MixVal/WikiText disagreement: the learned prior improves both. The column
       previously read as "MixVal" is training FLOPs (learned prior costs ~0.5% more).
     - Gains are small and single-seed; mark "experimental".
   Related, killed: "Closing the Loop" (2610.00673, >=1.4B, 64 GPUs) - see section F.

4. [C] LightMTP: Lightweight Latent Multi-Token Prediction
   arXiv 2610.06031 (2026-10-05) - Tamara Czinczoll, Julie Kallini, Gerard de Melo, Chen Shani
   https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.06031
   What: MTP whose future-token targets are the model's OWN intermediate hidden states (stop-grad, from
     ~2/3 depth) at future positions, predicted by a small 2-layer GELU MLP head with a cosine loss.
     No extra vocabulary projection, no extra logits. Head is discarded after training (no inference
     change). +0.3% (single-offset) to +0.9% (multi-offset) params at 24 layers.
   Why it matters for Quark: the plan's MTP is penalized at small scale and standard MTP heads add
     vocab-sized logits (a known 8 GB problem). This is the only MTP variant seen that adds almost no
     memory, and it did NOT cost LM quality in the paper.
   Evidence: FineWeb-Edu, nanochat recipe (12x scaling params in tokens). Main runs 24-layer ~1.4-1.5B;
     appendix has 12-layer ~286M. Byte-level PPL 1.683 vs 1.683 for NTP (no loss); CORE avg 40.30 vs
     40.43 (flat); gains only on Countdown (46.4 -> 56.7%) and MBPP-finetuned pass@10 (15.2 -> 23.3;
     MBPP runs use a separate model pretrained with 10% Python in the mix).
     Gloeckle-style and DeepSeek-style MTP baselines (the authors' reimplementations) were worse on LM.
   Fit for Quark: "head:mtp_latent". Treat as a regularizer/auxiliary objective, not a quality win.
   Watch for:
     - Smallest model is d12: 286M total but only 110M non-embedding ("scaling") params, so ~1-10x our
       size, not 3-30x. [corrected] The paper does NOT say the effect is unclear below 3B; it argues
       LightMTP is useful at <3B where earlier MTP hurt. Still untested at 10-100M; keep the "scale" soft rule.
     - bf16 autocast with fp32 logits/loss on H100s; T4/fp16 not tested. Cosine loss on normalized
       targets should be fp32. (FlashAttention is not mentioned in the paper text.) [corrected]
     - Variance regularizer improved probing but HURT LM benchmarks; leave it off.
     - Parameter counts in text vs tables are inconsistent; no code link seen.

5. [A] ORCA: Annealed Spectral Conditioning Optimizer
   arXiv 2610.06116 (2026-10-05) - Yuanshi Liu, Boyuan Jiang, Liang Hou, et al.
   https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.06116   (paper licensed CC BY-NC-SA 4.0)
   What: Muon plus a soft orthogonality penalty on hidden matrices for the first tau = 25% of steps,
     then switched off (plain Muon afterwards):
       W <- W - eta*U - eta*lambda*W - eta*gamma*(W W^T - I) W,   gamma = 1
     Two extra matmuls per matrix per step while active (~8% more matmuls than 8-step Newton-Schulz),
     overhead vanishes after release. Embeddings / head / 1-D params stay on AdamW.
   Evidence: LLaMA 130M, C4, seq 1024, batch 512 (~26B tokens), WSD (last 15% decay to 0.1x).
     Val loss: Adam 2.8808, Muon 2.8505, ORCA 2.8118 (4 seeds); LR-sweep winners Muon 2.8460 vs
     ORCA 2.8106. Also 350M (-0.019 vs Muon), 1.3B (-0.024), Qwen3 160M/0.6B, MoE 0.8B/8B.
     The same shaping applied to Adam and SOAP lowers their loss by ~0.04 too.
   Fit for Quark: "optim:orca" next to Muon in the Phase-2 optimizer slot. Pure matmuls (no SVD),
     no kernels, extra memory ~0 (paper: +0.25 GB at 8B).
   Watch for:
     - 130M is the smallest size; token budgets are 25-105B vs our ~0.2-2B. tau, gamma and LR must be
       re-tuned at our scale (paper's good range for tau: 0.10-0.55 at 130M).
     - bf16/fp16 not discussed. On the T4 the Newton-Schulz precision issue already in the plan applies
       (and the Gram term W W^T should be fp32). The AdamW-with-shaping variant may be the safe T4 path.
     - Most ablations are single-run at 130M; the "1.66x token efficiency" figure comes from a
       non-retuned continuation with 41.7% shaping - weak evidence, ignore it.
     - Official code not seen (kernels for batched same-shape matrices are mentioned, not released).

6. [B] BulkBoost: "Does Muon Need Fine-Grained Spectral Shaping?"
   arXiv 2610.07497 (2026-10-05) - Meher Chaitanya, Tianyi Zhou, Aristides Gionis
   https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.07497
   What: keep Muon's top singular directions at gain 1 ("spike" band, BB-Static: top 5%) and amplify the
     rest by rho = 4 ("bulk" band), then rescale so the update's Frobenius norm matches plain Muon.
     SVD-free: Polar Express for the polar factor plus warm-started block power iteration (q = 6).
   Evidence: this is one of the few papers tested BELOW 100M. Pythia 14M / 31M / 70M / 160M / 410M,
     continued pretraining (not from scratch) ~100M tokens each, seq 1024, 30 model x corpus settings
     (5 sizes x 6 corpora: FineMath-4, FineMath-3, CodeParrot, Wiki-de/zh/ar), 3 seeds. BB-Static
     beats Muon in 23/30 (2 ties); BB-Adaptive 24/30. Mean loss gain vs Muon only ~0.07% of the starting
     loss. Per-setting differences are tiny (e.g. Muon 3.086 vs BB-Adaptive 3.085 at 14M). [corrected]
   Fit for Quark: "optim:muon+bulkboost" as an ablation next to ORCA, not instead of it.
   Watch for:
     - Step time ~1.19-1.27x Muon (static) / 1.36-1.50x (adaptive, needs two half-batch gradients).
     - Continued pretraining from converged (AdamW-trained) Pythia checkpoints only; no from-scratch run.
     - The "~7.8% / ~8.0% tokens saved" averages only the settings and seeds that beat Muon, so it is
       biased upward. Plain Muon was best on several corpora at 160M. [corrected]
     - Best fraction varies by setting; the "best fraction" row is tuned on reported loss (not fair).
     - Precision and code not stated.

7. [C] The Best Optimizer Depends on Batch Size (planner guidance, not a module)
   arXiv 2610.08975 (2026-10-06) - Xingyu Dang, Kaiyue Wen, Sadhika Malladi
   https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.08975
   What: after per-batch-size tuning, the best LM-pretraining optimizer changes with batch size, and no
     single Muon batch-size scaling rule works across settings.
   Evidence: Modded-NanoGPT benchmark (~85M hidden-matrix params in the branching experiments), ~1.7B
     tokens, batches 128K / 512K / 1M / 2M tokens; Adam, Lion, Muon, SOAP, Shampoo. SOAP best from
     128K to 1M (up to 0.012 nats ahead); Shampoo best at 2M; Muon vs Shampoo within +-0.004 nats.
     Best LM scaling rule: keep LR, scale matrix weight decay linearly (1M and 2M). Keep Adam beta1 /
     Muon momentum fixed when changing batch size. Effect shrinks after LR decay.
   Fit for Quark: planner rule/guard. Do not hard-code "Muon > AdamW"; make the optimizer prior depend
     on effective batch size, and re-verify when grad-accum changes it.
   Watch for:
     - Smallest batch tested is 128K tokens; ours is ~8-65K per step unless we accumulate. The rules are
       NOT shown to hold at our batch sizes; gaps are tiny (0.0003-0.012 nats) and near seed noise.
     - Hardware/precision not seen. SOAP's state is 2m^2+2n^2 per matrix (7.6 GB optimizer state at 350M
       in another paper's table), so SOAP likely only fits our 8 GB at <= ~100M params (my estimate).

8. [B] Learning Rate Transfer for Hybrid Transformer-SSM Architectures (also covers pure transformers)
   arXiv 2610.01172 (2026-10-01) - Jimin Seo, Gyubok Lee, Yeonsik Jo, et al.
   https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.01172
   What: "naive muP" + AdamW (beta2 0.95): embeddings N(0,1), hidden N(0,1/d_model), zero-init output
     head, hidden-layer LR = eta_base / d_model. Optimal base LR stayed at 1.0 across widths 256-1024
     for all 7 architectures INCLUDING a LLaMA-style pure transformer (0.0% transfer gap).
     Standard parameterization incurred a 1.1-8.7% penalty (FineWeb-Edu) / 1.9% (Nemotron-H) when
     the proxy LR was reused.
   Evidence: proxy w=256, depth 8 (~58M) -> target w=1024, depth 32 (~624M), but that depth+width test is
     on hybrids (MHMH/HHHH); the pure-transformer (TT) evidence is width 256-1024 only. Also 8x width
     to 1.24B. [corrected]
     Short sweeps 2k steps on WikiText-2 (seq 128, batch 64); validated 20k steps on WikiText-103;
     FineWeb-Edu proxy (w=256, d=16) transferred to a 5B-token run.
   Fit for Quark: planner/estimator. Gives the LR-selection rule the plan lacks: sweep LR once at a
     w=256 proxy (cheap on the 3070 Ti), transfer up with eta/d_model, run one verification at target
     width. d_model 256-1024 is exactly Quark's range. Also supports comparing candidates fairly
     (same-tuned LR per architecture).
   Watch for:
     - AdamW only: Muon with its recommended scaling did NOT transfer in this paper (Sophia did).
       Muon/ORCA/BulkBoost still need their own LR sweeps.
     - Several optima sat at the TOP of the LR grid (1.0); extend the grid.
     - The coordinate check FAILS on hybrids yet transfer holds, so do not use it as a go/no-go test.
     - Inconsistencies: FineWeb-Edu run listed as 5.0B vs 5.5B tokens; SP variance written as 1/sqrt(d)
       and 1/d. Code not seen. GPUs were 24-80 GB; memory not addressed.

9. [C] Why Does Adaptive Batching Help LLM Pretraining? (Batch-size schedule)
   arXiv 2610.02355 (2026-10-01) - Arda Fazla, Antesh Upadhyay, Ege C. Kaya
   https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.02355
   What: batch grows in powers of two: k_t = 2^ceil(log2(1 + (B^2/G^2) * ||x_t - x_0||^a)), batch
     b_t = k_t * b_0, LR multiplied by sqrt(k_t) for Adam. Needs only the distance from the initial
     weights and three noise constants (B, G, a) fitted once by a perturbation measurement pass at init.
   Evidence: OLMo2 100M / 600M / 1B on C4, ~20 tokens/param, AdamW, 5% warmup + cosine. At 100M
     (seq 2048): batch 32 -> val 3.4609 +- 0.0037 in 30,518 steps; batch 512 -> 3.5139 in 1,908;
     adaptive -> 3.4347 +- 0.0085 in 2,673 steps. 3 seeds. [corrected] The paper reports optimizer-step
     counts (8.76% of small-batch steps at 100M), not wall-clock; no "20-40% wall-clock" figure was found.
   Fit for Quark: Phase-2 "schedule" extension / trainer option for grad-accum count. Quality gain at
     equal tokens (here ~0.026 nats vs the small batch) is the part that can transfer.
   Watch for:
     - The wall-clock saving comes from parallel hardware. With one GPU and grad accumulation, tokens/sec
       is fixed, so expect fewer optimizer steps but NOT proportionally less time. Test at equal tokens.
     - Needs a copy of x_0 (+1 param copy, small at <=100M); checkpoint must store x_0 and (B,G,a).
     - Tested only with AdamW + cosine (WSD compatible in principle, untested); resume not discussed.
     - Only the 100M model is within ~2x of our range; tokens/budget text has a typo-level inconsistency.

10. [A] Terminal Shrinkage Averaging (TSA)
    arXiv 2609.25482 (2026-09-21) - Adam Ousherovitch, Yixin Wang - https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.25482
    What: return a blend of the final weights and the average of the last K checkpoints:
        theta_hat = (1 - alpha) * theta_T + alpha * mean_j theta_{T - j*s}
      Main setting K = 8, spacing s = 32 steps, alpha = 0.55. Optimizer is untouched; it is a
      post-hoc change to what you return. Paper's key finding: averaging changes the best terminal-LR
      floor - a higher floor HURTS the raw final iterate but HELPS the TSA result (interaction
      0.0014 BPB at a 10% floor, 0.0046 at 15%).
    Evidence: NanoChat depth-12 (286M params, seq 512, 3,000 steps, ~98M tokens), Muon + AdamW; also
      depth-22 at 9 tokens/param (3 seeds; gain only ~0.0007 BPB, 1 of 3 seeds clears the CORE threshold -
      the authors call it preliminary). Gains are small.
    Fit for Quark: costs almost nothing and matches the plan's "keep last K checkpoints" design. Implement
      as a post-training "export averaged checkpoint" step (and a trainer option to save a short burst of
      checkpoints at the end). The interaction with the LR floor is relevant to the plan's
      "extend a WSD run from a pre-decay checkpoint" idea.
    Watch for:
      - Not tested with WSD vs cosine explicitly (NanoChat's native cooldown with a floor clamp).
      - Stores K snapshots (bf16 in the paper): negligible at 10-100M params, but at 100M x 8 x 2 B
        = 1.6 GB, check against Drive/disk limits.
      - [corrected] Not Muon-only: the floor x TSA interaction also replicates under pure AdamW (smaller:
        0.0008 / 0.0025 BPB at 10% / 15% floors, positive in 5/5 runs). Depth-12 TSA gain itself is
        ~0.0007 BPB. Hardware 8xH100; no code for TSA itself seen.
      - Keep the plan's rule of holding fp32 weights; averaging in bf16 snapshots loses precision.

F. REVIEWED AND KILLED / NOT SELECTED (with reasons)
======================================================================

Hard kills (would not work for our project or GPUs as published):

- 2610.05126  Selecting Repetition Counts Across Model Scales in Data-Constrained Pretraining.
  Procedure needs 3 sizes x 7 repetition counts x 3 seeds = 63 runs per corpus (up to 6B tokens each),
  to predict choices for 200M-520M targets; setup assumes a 100M-token target pool mixed 80/20 with a
  generic stream, which does not map to a single fixed dataset like TinyStories. Takeaway worth keeping
  (best repetition count shifts with model size; reusing the small model's choice failed) but the
  method is out of budget.
- 2610.06963  WavePrune: One period is often enough for RoPE.
  Per-channel windowing (each RoPE channel limited to its first period) is not expressible as a single
  SDPA mask; the paper's speed/benefit comes from custom FlashAttention-2-based kernels (bf16, A100),
  and a stock-SDPA route would be an O(T^2) eager path with no speed gain. Pretraining tests are GPT-2
  117M-774M at ctx 2048 (relevant scale) but the gain targets long-context extrapolation, which DaRoPE/HoPE
  already cover with SDPA-compatible code. Llama3.1-8B regressed in its own table.
- 2609.34681  SOLAR: a state-driven online LR scheduler (PPO controller).
  Adds a PPO actor-critic into the training loop with per-group LR control, its own checkpoint-abort
  hook, ~15 new hyperparameters, no code seen, and no statement about resuming policy state after an
  interruption (Colab). Mostly single-seed; hyperparameters were chosen once at 60M. Conflicts with the
  plan's deterministic WSD "early cooldown on plateau" logic.
- 2610.00673  Closing the Loop: practical training recipes for looped LMs.
  Smallest model 1.4B, 310B tokens from scratch, 64 GPUs/FSDP/FlashAttention-3. Nothing below 500M.
  Portable observations only (fixed T=4 instead of 8, final RMSNorm before the head matters, post-norm and
  sandwich-norm failed, exit-gate collapse is invisible in LM loss).
- 2610.04204  Clean / Q-Clean: second-order training at linear memory (Nystrom sketching).
  Only 350M and 1.3B on 4xH100, ranks tuned at 256 (as large as our widths), precision of QR/Cholesky/
  SVD steps unstated (T4 risk), no code seen. Still trails SOAP in PPL, uses more memory than Muon.
  Memory saving is irrelevant at <=100M where SOAP/Muon already fit.
- 2609.39813  Backward-State Policy Is Part of the Learning Algorithm.
  Concerns FP8/FP4 backward rounding reuse (TransformerEngine on Hopper/Blackwell, 390M and 1.5B).
  No fp16/GradScaler or bf16-autocast result; nothing actionable for our precision modes.
- 2610.04002  Do Language Models Need a Trainable Input Embedding Table?
  1.7B, 100B tokens, one run per variant, trainable embedding was better on most benchmarks and the
  output head stays a full VxD matrix. At small vocab (4-8k) the saved table is only a few M params;
  no evidence below 500M.
- 2610.10154  HySPE: positional encoding via symplectic dual shears.
  Single author, tests at 4.8M byte-level and one 51M WikiText run, RoPE-only baseline (no YaRN or
  DaRoPE), in-domain PPL 4.3% worse than RoPE, not a q/k drop-in (needs eigenbasis projections and
  block-wise SDPA calls), exponent bounds fail in fp16 (only fp32/bf16 tested).
- 2610.04867  Greedy Local Learning for LM pretraining.
  Measurement study, not a method: still +0.04 to +0.13 nats worse than end-to-end, extra auxiliary-head
  FLOPs (+39% to +97%), smallest model 125M, single seed, stage footprint at 400M already above 8 GB.
- 2610.06578  DGA-Muon.
  1.38B only, 8xA100, no seeds, no plain-Muon end-to-end comparison, small-scale ablations omitted.
- 2609.37535  Why Adaptive Optimizers Underestimate Rare Tokens.
  Theory is plausible but the LM experiment is a 64-dim MLP on a synthetic Markov chain, CPU, no seeds
  reported. Only cheap advice (higher beta2, check rare-token calibration) is usable.
- 2610.06387  Scaling Down the Scaling Laws.
  A single-author narrative literature review, not an empirical study; no fitted small-scale
  coefficients, no estimators. Not usable for the planner.

Not selected (feasible, but outranked or evidence below our scale):

- 2610.10975  Optimizing LLMs with Chained LMOs (TensorChain). Qwen3 0.6B / 1.7B only, 1M-4M-token
  batches, single seed, 7.8-12.8% token savings vs Muon, Newton-Schulz in bf16; no evidence <0.6B and
  ORCA is better evidenced at 130M. Revisit if ORCA doesn't pan out.
- 2610.10114  Mechanics of Long-Context Hybrid Models, Part 1.1 (hybrid position). 376M-3B, 50B tokens,
  8xH200; the training-free extrapolation (SWLA) needs FLA gated-linear-attention kernels. Design hints
